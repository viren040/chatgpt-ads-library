Counts represent reviewed placements in this library. Select an advertiser to inspect its creatives and evidence.

What this library proves

Each record proves that the displayed advertiser and creative appeared as a paid placement in one captured session at the recorded time. A reviewer confirmed the paid disclosure, advertiser identity, and matching screenshot.

Analyze the complete dataset

Download placements.csv for one row per reviewed placement, prompt-observations.csv for every settled complete-panel prompt including zero-ad outcomes, or library.json for nested advertiser, experiment, and evidence data. The prompt dataset retains a pseudonymous conversation ID and turn index so same-conversation changes can be analyzed without publishing account or session identifiers. Hashes for the datasets and every screenshot are recorded in manifest.json.

How to compare prompt categories

The category and industry fields describe the prompt context where an ad appeared. They do not classify the advertiser or creative. Cross-category ads remain visible evidence of what the session displayed.

  1. Choose a fixed prompt category and prompt panel.
  2. Keep country, account tier, login state, language, viewport, browser platform, and collection version consistent.
  3. Compare completed turns, paid-placement rate, its 95% interval, creative variants, and distinct advertisers.
  4. Treat fewer than eight observations as exploratory, 8–19 as directional, and at least 20 as established evidence.
  5. Treat results as observed availability. They do not reveal spend, bids, targeting settings, conversions, or whether a campaign is still active.

Read the denominator

Appearance rates use complete shard panels containing both ad and zero-ad turns. Positive-only historical migrations remain searchable as evidence but are excluded from rate calculations.

Evidence rules

  • Organic brand mentions do not count.
  • A paid card must belong to the current completed answer.
  • Every published record has a content-addressed screenshot.
  • Workers never click paid placements.
